# Cover letter template

Print or paste into an email. Fill in the [brackets]. Delete this heading and the notes. Keep it to **one page**. Attach the one-page summary and the nightly log from [`log-sheet.md`](log-sheet.md).

> **Unverified seed — no field trial yet.** This template has not been used with a real office. Change it freely.
> **No decibel (dB) numbers.** Do not add one. A phone cannot give a number you can defend.

**Subject: Low-frequency hum at night — request for review — [your street or neighborhood]**

Dear [Name / "Sir or Madam"],

I live at [address]. For about [how long], I have heard a low hum at night. It keeps me awake. I am writing to ask your office to review the attached record.

**What I did.** On [number] nights between [first date] and [last date], I recorded sound with a phone and looked at which pitches were in it. I used [phone model], [app and version], placed the same way each night.

**What I found.** A steady tone near [___] Hz was detected from about [start time] to [stop time] on [___] of [___] monitored nights. The attached one-page summary and nightly log show the details.

**What this record is not.** It does not give a loudness level. A phone cannot do that reliably. It does not say what causes the hum. It does not say any rule was broken. It is a screening record only.

**What I am asking.** Please:

1. Confirm in writing that you received this, and give me a case or ticket number.
2. Tell me which office handles low-frequency noise complaints, if it is not yours.
3. Tell me which sound measurement method and filter (A-weighted, C-weighted, or none) your rules use for a complaint like this.
4. Tell me whether your office can measure at night, over more than one night, and when.
5. Tell me who I should contact next if you cannot help.

Thank you for your time. I can share more records or let you listen on site at a time that works for you.

Sincerely,

[Your name]

**Attached:** One-page summary · Seven-night log
